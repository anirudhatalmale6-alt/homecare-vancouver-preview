Where we work

Across Metro Vancouver, community by community

Caregiving is a local job. Bridge traffic, a narrow East Van street with no parking, a Burnaby tower with loading-bay rules, a Tsawwassen address that is forty minutes from anywhere — all of it decides whether a 7am shift actually starts at 7am. Below is every community we cover, grouped the way the health system groups them, because which health authority you fall under changes who you phone for a publicly funded assessment.

Vancouver Coastal Health

Publicly funded home support in these communities is assessed by Vancouver Coastal Health. What we do is private, so no assessment or waiting list is involved — but it is worth knowing which door is which.

  • VancouverBridges and one-way streets decide whether a shift starts on time. We schedule the West End and East Vancouver very differently for that reason.
  • North VancouverSteep driveways, long wet winters and a lot of split-level houses. Getting someone safely from the front door to a car is half the job here.
  • West VancouverLarge houses that people very much intend to stay in. Live-in and overnight cover comes up more often here than anywhere else we work.
  • RichmondFlat, spread out and well served by road. Straightforward to cover, and family language and cultural preferences are worth raising on the first call.

Fraser Health

East of Boundary Road the public side is run by Fraser Health. Same point: we are the private option, and we will tell you honestly if the public route would serve you better.

  • BurnabyA great deal of high-rise living. Fobs, visitor parking and loading-bay booking get sorted before the first shift, not on the morning of it.
  • New WestminsterOlder houses with steep front steps, and Royal Columbian discharging into them. The first week home is what we are usually called about.
  • CoquitlamSpread out, and transit thins above the highway. Longer, less frequent visits usually work better here than short daily ones.
  • Port CoquitlamCompact and easy to reach, which makes short daily visits practical — a morning routine, a meal, a check before bed.
  • Port MoodySmall enough that the same caregiver can realistically cover a whole week, which is the single thing that makes home care work.
  • DeltaLadner and Tsawwassen are a genuine drive from the rest of the region, so we block shifts rather than send someone across the bridge twice a day.
  • SurreyThe largest area we cover, and the most variable. Tell us the neighbourhood on the first call — Newton and South Surrey are not the same job.
  • White RockAn older population and a lot of stairs down to the water. Mobility support and falls prevention dominate the calls we get here.
  • LangleyCity and Township are quite different to schedule. Rural addresses are fine, they just need a little more notice.
  • Maple RidgeLong distances between addresses. We plan routes rather than slots, so a caregiver is not spending the day in a car.
  • Pitt MeadowsSmall, and usually covered alongside Maple Ridge by the same caregiver, which keeps the face at the door consistent.
  • MissionThe far edge of where we go. We will say plainly on the phone whether we can staff it properly rather than promise and reschedule.

How we help

Four ways families usually start

Most people call us in the middle of something — a fall, a discharge date, a parent who has quietly stopped cooking. You do not need to know which service you want. Describe the week and we will tell you what it would take to cover it.

01

A few hours, a few days a week

Company, a proper meal, a shower done safely, a drive to an appointment at VGH. Often the version that keeps someone at home for years longer than expected.

02

Overnight and waking nights

For the households where nobody has slept properly in a month. A caregiver stays through the night so the family can stop listening for footsteps.

03

Live-in support

Someone in the home full time, with their own room. Usually where the alternative on the table is a care facility nobody in the family actually wants.

04

Coming home from hospital

The first two weeks after a discharge are where most readmissions start. We cover them — medications, mobility, the follow-up appointments.

05

Respite for the family carer

You have been doing it all yourself. We take some of the week so you can go back to being a daughter or a husband instead of staff.

06

Memory and dementia support

Familiar routines, familiar faces, and a caregiver who understands that the same question at 4pm every day is not a problem to be fixed.

Paying for it

How home care is actually funded in British Columbia

This is the question everybody wants to ask and nobody quite does. Here is the plain version, with no obligation attached to reading it.

  • Publicly subsidisedYour health authority assesses the person and can arrange subsidised home support, with a client rate set by income. West of Boundary Road that is Vancouver Coastal Health; east of it, Fraser Health. Ask for a home care assessment.
  • Privately paidYou arrange and pay directly. Nobody assesses you, nothing is rationed, and you choose the hours and the caregiver. This is what we do.
  • Extended health and insuranceSome employer plans, long-term care policies and ICBC or WorkSafeBC claims will fund attendant care. Worth checking the policy before assuming not.
  • Veterans Affairs CanadaThe Veterans Independence Program contributes towards housekeeping and personal care for eligible veterans. Details are on the Veterans Affairs Canada site.

The honest answer on price

Hourly private care in Metro Vancouver is not cheap, and any agency quoting you a rate before asking a single question about the person is quoting a number, not a plan. Cost depends on how many hours, what time of day, whether nights are waking or sleeping, and whether the care needs a nurse rather than a caregiver.

Call and describe the situation. We will give you a real figure for the week you have described, and if publicly funded support would serve you better, we will say so.

Questions we get asked

Before you call

How quickly can care start?

For daytime hours across most of the region, often within 24 to 48 hours. Overnight and live-in placements take a little longer because matching the person matters more than filling the slot. If you are working to a discharge date, tell us the date on the first call.

Is there a minimum number of hours?

Yes, most agencies have one, and we will tell you ours plainly on the phone rather than burying it in a contract. It exists because a caregiver crossing the city for a 45-minute visit is not a job anyone will keep.

Do we get the same caregiver each time?

That is the aim, and it is the single biggest thing that makes home care work — particularly with memory loss. Holidays and illness happen, but consistency is the plan, not the exception.

Which cities do you actually cover?

Vancouver, North and West Vancouver, Richmond, Burnaby, New Westminster, Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, Delta, Surrey, White Rock, Langley, Maple Ridge, Pitt Meadows and Mission. If your address sits at the edge of that, say so on the first call — we would rather be honest about staffing than promise and then reschedule you.

What if we only need someone temporarily?

That is completely normal. Post-surgery cover, a family carer going away for two weeks, a difficult winter — short arrangements are a large part of what we do.
