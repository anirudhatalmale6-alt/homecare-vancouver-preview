Managing Aggressive Behaviour in Elderly at Home

AN All Nursing Health Services Care Team Published February 1, 2025 6 min read

In this article

  • What challenging behaviour looks like
  • Common underlying causes
  • Responding in the moment
  • Preventive steps between episodes
  • When to seek additional support

Aggressive or disruptive behaviour can be one of the most difficult aspects of caring for a loved one with dementia. It's important to remember that this behaviour is a symptom of the condition, not a reflection of who the person truly is — and understanding what's driving it is the first step toward responding effectively.

What challenging behaviour looks like

Challenging behaviour can include verbal outbursts, physical resistance to care, agitation, or disruptiveness that impacts day-to-day activities. These episodes can be distressing for both the person experiencing them and the family or caregiver nearby, particularly when they seem to come without warning.

Common underlying causes

Aggressive behaviour in dementia is often a form of communication when verbal expression has become difficult. Common triggers include:

  • Physical discomfort, pain or illness that can't be clearly communicated
  • Overstimulation from noise, crowds or unfamiliar environments
  • Fear or confusion about an unfamiliar task or person
  • Frustration from being unable to complete a task independently
  • Fatigue or disrupted sleep patterns

Responding in the moment

When aggression does occur, staying calm is the most powerful tool available. Avoid arguing or trying to reason through the behaviour in the moment — this often escalates rather than resolves it. Instead, give the person space, lower your voice, and try to identify what immediate need or discomfort might be driving the reaction.

Redirecting attention to a different activity or environment can also help de-escalate a situation once immediate safety is established.

Aggressive behaviour in dementia is usually communication, not character — understanding the cause is the first step to responding well.

Preventive steps between episodes

Reducing the frequency of challenging behaviour often comes down to prevention: maintaining a consistent daily routine, minimizing overstimulating environments, ensuring adequate rest, and addressing pain or discomfort proactively with a physician. A calm, predictable environment reduces the likelihood of triggers arising in the first place.

When to seek additional support

If aggressive behaviour is frequent, intense, or poses a safety risk, it's worth involving a healthcare professional. A Registered Nurse can help assess whether an underlying medical issue is contributing to the behaviour, and specially trained caregivers can provide consistent, structured support that reduces the burden on family members trying to manage these episodes alone.
