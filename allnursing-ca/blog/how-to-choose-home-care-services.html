How to Choose Home Care Services: A Consumer Guide

AN All Nursing Health Services Care Team Published February 1, 2025 5 min read

In this article

  • Define the level of care needed
  • Questions to ask a provider
  • Red flags to watch for

Choosing a home care provider for a loved one is a decision that deserves careful thought — and a little bit of a roadmap. Here's how to approach the search methodically, so you land on a provider that's genuinely the right fit.

Start with a doctor's guidance

The first step to finding the right home care service provider is to ask a doctor or other healthcare provider what kind of support is actually needed. Some seniors only require casual companionship or occasional help with errands, while others need consistent personal care or clinical nursing support. Getting clarity here up front will save time narrowing down providers later.

Define the level of care needed

Home care needs generally fall into a few broad categories:

  • Companionship and light assistance — conversation, errands, light housekeeping
  • Personal care — bathing, dressing, grooming and hygiene support
  • Skilled nursing — wound care, medication administration, clinical monitoring
  • Round-the-clock or live-in care — continuous supervision and support

Being specific about which category (or combination) applies will help you compare providers on an apples-to-apples basis.

Questions to ask a provider

Once you've narrowed down a few candidates, a short list of questions can reveal a lot:

  • Are caregivers screened, bonded and insured?
  • Is there a Registered Nurse involved in building and supervising the care plan?
  • How are caregivers matched to clients, and is backup coverage available?
  • Is there a free, no-obligation assessment before committing to a plan?
  • How is billing structured, and is it transparent and itemized?
The right provider will welcome these questions — hesitancy or vague answers are worth taking seriously.

Red flags to watch for

Be cautious of providers who are unwilling to provide an in-home assessment before starting care, who can't clearly explain how caregivers are vetted, or who are vague about pricing and contract terms. A reputable agency will be transparent on all of these points from the first conversation.

Starting with a trial period

Even after doing your research, it's reasonable to start with a smaller initial commitment — a few hours a week, for example — before expanding to a larger care plan. This gives your family a chance to evaluate fit without a long-term commitment right away, and a good provider should be comfortable starting small and growing the relationship over time.
